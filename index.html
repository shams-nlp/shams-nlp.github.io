Abstract

Levantine Arabic (LA) is spoken by tens of millions of people, creating a pressing need for shared benchmarks to evaluate LA speech-language technologies. Evaluating such technology is particularly challenging given LA’s internal diversity and its opaque and non-standardized orthography.

We present SHAMS (SHami Annotated Multi-dialect Speech), a benchmark comprising 1,300 utterances drawn from open audio corpora, balanced across five LA varieties (Urban and Rural Palestinian, and Urban Jordanian, Lebanese, and Syrian). Each utterance is represented across four aligned tiers: audio, unvocalized orthography, diacritized text, and phonetic transcription. This structure supports evaluation of various downstream tasks such as diacritization, grapheme-to-phoneme conversion, automatic speech recognition, and audio-to-phoneme, grounded in audio and stratified by variety. We benchmark open and proprietary models across these tasks to demonstrate the utility of this benchmark for measuring progress across LA.

The gap

Even fully diacritized orthography leaves dialectal pronunciation underspecified: for example, the realization of ق is not recoverable from the script.

Automating this transcription could relieve the bottleneck, but for LA there has been no way to measure how well current systems perform the task.

Diacritization has been studied extensively for MSA, with more limited work on G2P and Arabic dialects. These efforts address individual tasks in isolation, and neither source corpus provides diacritics, a phonetic tier, or variety-level stratification; our novel contribution is an evaluation that ties diacritization, G2P, ASR, and A2P together over the same utterances, grounded in audio and stratified by LA variety.

Four aligned tiers

Each utterance in SHAMS includes four aligned tiers:

  1. (i)the audio segment;
  2. (ii)unvocalized Arabic orthography, the consonantal text as conventionally written, corrected when needed to match the recording; بقدر أروح بكرا
  3. (iii)fully diacritized Arabic, the same text with conventional diacritics matching the pronunciation in the audio; بَقدَر أَرُوح بُكرَا
  4. (iv)a phonetic transcription in a Semitic transcription system. bakdar ʾarūḥ bučra

Four tasks

Diacritization (ii) → (iii) · DER
Restoring vowel and gemination marks.
Grapheme-to-phoneme conversion (G2P) (ii) → (iv) · CER
Mapping orthography to phonetic transcription.
Automatic speech recognition (ASR) (i) → (ii) · WER, CER
Transcribing audio to text.
Audio-to-phoneme conversion (A2P) (i) → (iv) · CER
Transcribing audio to phonetic transcription.

Composition

SHAMS comprises 1,300 utterances totaling roughly 15,500 words and just over two hours of speech, spread across five Levantine varieties. The benchmark is built on the Levantine portions of two open multi-dialectal Arabic speech corpora, Casablanca and LangMedia.

How to use

Data: huggingface.co/datasets/shams-nlp/shams.
Evaluation code and model outputs: github.com/bensapirstein/shams.

Every score is computed after an evaluation-time normalization, applied symmetrically to references and hypotheses, at two levels. The strict tier scores the output as written; the neutral tier additionally folds spelling and notation that carry no phonemic contrast in Levantine.
